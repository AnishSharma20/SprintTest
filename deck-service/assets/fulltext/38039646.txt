A B S T R A C T  
Background: The potential role of krill oil (KO) supplementation on cardiovascular health are inconsistent in 
several clinical trials. Therefore, our present meta-analysis aimed to systematically evaluate the impacts of 
supplementation of KO on cardiovascular disease risk factors (CVDRFs). 
Methods: Intervention trials assessing KO supplementation on cardiovascular disease (CVD) outcomes were 
systematically retrieved for pooling. The primary outcome was lipid prole. Secondary outcomes were consisted 
by blood pressure, glycemic indices, body composition together with inammatory markers. We synthesized the 
effect sizes with 95% condence intervals and weighted mean difference. To explore the heterogeneity source, 
we employed meta-regression and subgroup analysis. Quality assessment, publication bias, sensitivity-analysis 
and the certainty of evidence were also carried out. 
Results: We included 14 trials (18 treatment arms) with 1458 participants. KO supplementation had benecial 
effects on total cholesterol (

management strategies. In future, studies should pay attention to the relationships of KO intake with the inci-
dence of CVD events or all-cause mortality.

4. Discussion 
According to the literature from RCTs and epidemiology, natural 
dietary products and nutraceutical therapies signicantly affect CVD 
prevention and atherosclerosis-mediated cardiovascular events for 
many reasons, such as poor adherence to medications, drug contrain-
dications, various side effects, or individual preference for natural or 
alternative therapies [
,
]. In the present study, we summarized the 
39 40
available scientic data on the benecial effects of KO ingestion on the 
biomarkers associated with CVD risk acquired in RCTs. For statistical 
pooling, we included 14 trials (18 treatment arms) with 1458 in-
dividuals, and found that KO positively affects TC, LDL-C as well as TG. 
Despite of that, compared to control group, the HDL-C, SBP, DBP, FBG, 
Insulin, HOMA-IR, BW, BMI, WC, HC, and CRP are not changed after KO 
supplementation treatment. KO demonstrated a favorable tolerability 
prole, showing no substantial difference in adverse events between KO 
and control treatments. According to our subgroup analysis, KO intake 
dramatically suppressed TC, LDL-C as well as TG levels in the following 
subgroups: KO < 3 g/d, mean BMI < 28 kg/m2, duration <8 weeks, and 
crossover design. In addition, the subgroup analysis ndings showed 
that KO consumption lowered TG levels in trials with duration 
8 
≥
weeks, dose
3 g/d, and parallel design with statistical signicance. Our 
≥
ndings may provide novel insight that could be integrated into dietary 
strategies aimed at preventing atherosclerosis development, promoting 
cardiovascular health in human beings.

disease. The escalated LDL-cholesterol levels may cause plaque forma-
tion through macrophage foam cells induced by LDL-cholesterol 
oxidation, resulting in atherosclerosis [
]. As a result, the mainte-
41

atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease is dependent on cholesterol ho-
meostasis [
]. Epidemiological and prospective studies have robustly 
42

proportional decrease in cardiovascular disease events corresponding to 
each 1% decrease in LDL-C levels [
]. In accordance with the updated 
43

regarding cholesterol testing and management, the primary objective of 
drug therapy prioritizes progressively lower LDL-C levels for the treat-
ment of cholesterol and prevention of cardiovascular disease [
]. As 
43

pharmacological therapy in patients with dyslipidemia. Although the 
exact mechanisms responsible for the lipid-lowering impacts of KO

5. Limitation 
Although the present meta-analysis provides useful implications for 
clinical practice, some certain limitations that should be mentioned. 
First, a majority of the included trials carried out in relatively heterog-
enous populations, including healthy participants, subjects with hy-
pertension, hypercholesterolemic, and knee osteoarthritis or knee pain. 
Thus, since we did not have access to the original data from the studies 
reviewed, it was not possible to fully establish the impact of KO on 
cardiovascular disease risk factors in different populations through 
subgroup analyses. Second, only a small number of trials were available 
for some secondary outcomes such as HC, WC, Insulin, HbA1c, HOMA- 
IR and CRP. Therefore, these ndings must be interpreted cautiously, 
and further more large-scale, high-quality clinical studies are required to 
reach more condent conclusions. Third, the recruited subjects could 
have various background habits of consumptions, medications, and 
lifestyles which may affect the lipid prole, blood pressure, and other 
CVD risk factors signicantly in individuals. Finally, we found signi-
cant heterogeneity among trials for the TG outcome (I2 = 85%). How-
ever, sensitivity, meta-regression, and a priori subgroup analyses did not 
completely solve the sources of heterogeneity. 
6. Conclusions 
KO supplementation could signicantly improve lipid metabolism by 
decreasing the TC, LDL-C and TG levels. However, KO supplementation 
show no benecial effect in SBP, DBP, FBG, Insulin, HbA1c, HOMA-IR, 
BW, BMI, WC, HC, or CRP. The effects of KO on lipid markers were 
affected by the trial design, supplement dosage, baseline BMI, and 
intervention duration. Our subgroup analyses showed that supplemen-
tation of kril oil decreased TC and TG in trials with daily KO dose <3 g/ 
d. Moreover, a reduction in LDL-C and TG were also observed in those 
with daily KO dose 
3 g. Our ndings might help healthcare practi-
≥
tioners and the general public better comprehend the evidence on the 
CVD preventive benets of KO supplementation. It should be noted that 
although certain benecial lipid-modifying effects were observed after 
KO supplementation, in future, studies should pay attention to re-
lationships of KO intake with the incidence of CVD events or all-cause 
mortality. Additionally, researches on a standardize KO supplementa-
tion protocol, including the dosing regimen and duration of adminis-
tration, are also of necessity. 
Statement of signicance 
Evidence regarding the potential role of krill oil (KO) supplementa-
tion on cardiovascular health in humans are inconsistent. The present 
updated meta-analysis was the most recent, critical, in-depth compara-
tive investigation, which systematically analyze published clinical evi-
dence and evaluate of the effects of KO supplementation in preventing or 
treating cardiovascular disease (CVD) in humans. The pooled result 
showed that KO supplementation could signicantly improve lipid 
metabolism by decreasing the total cholesterol, low-density lipoprotein 
cholesterol, and triglycerides levels. The present ndings might help
